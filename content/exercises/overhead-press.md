# Overhead Press

## Details

- **Category:** Vertical Push
- **Difficulty:** intermediate
- **Movement pattern:** push
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell, Kettlebell
- **Primary muscles:** Front Delts, Triceps
- **Secondary muscles:** Chest, Abs, Upper Back
- **Aliases:** dumbbell shoulder press

## Instructions

1. Stand tall with feet hip-width apart, holding dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing each other or slightly forward. Brace your core and squeeze your glutes.
2. Press the weights overhead until your arms are fully extended, biceps finishing roughly by your ears. Do not lean back.
3. Pause briefly at the top without shrugging your shoulders.
4. Lower the weights back to shoulder height with control. That is one rep.

## Cues

- Ribs down, glutes squeezed — no lean-back
- Press up and slightly in, biceps finishing by your ears
- Lower with control to the starting position every rep

## Common mistakes

- Arching the lower back to heave the weight up
- Shrugging the shoulders toward the ears at the top
- Pressing the weights out in front instead of overhead

## Safety

If you have shoulder impingement, keep the reps pain-free and stop well short of lockout if the top position pinches. Never press behind the neck.

## Longevity notes

Overhead strength is independence: putting luggage in the bin, reaching the top shelf, catching yourself in a fall. The standing press builds it while forcing the core and glutes to stabilize — a full-body investment from one movement. Keep the loads moderate and the reps clean; cranky shoulders come from grinding heavy singles, not from patient sub-maximal volume.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** Add the smallest available increment when you can complete 3 sets of 10 with no back arch at RPE 7 or less.
- **Progress to:** Barbell overhead press
- **Regress to:** Seated dumbbell press (back supported)
