# Dead Hang

## Details

- **Category:** Vertical Pull
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** pull
- **Equipment:** Other
- **Primary muscles:** Forearms, Lats
- **Secondary muscles:** Abs
- **Aliases:** passive hang

## Instructions

1. Grip an overhead bar with both hands, palms facing away, hands just outside shoulder-width.
2. Step off the support and let your body hang with straight arms, feet off the ground.
3. Keep your shoulders pulled slightly down away from your ears — don't shrug up.
4. Breathe calmly and hang for time. Step back onto the support to finish.

## Cues

- Shoulders down, away from ears
- Relaxed jaw, steady breathing
- Full grip, thumb wrapped

## Common mistakes

- Shrugging the shoulders up to the ears
- Holding your breath
- Swinging the body

## Safety

Skip or modify if you have an acute shoulder injury or uncontrolled high blood pressure. Stop if you feel sharp shoulder pain rather than muscular fatigue.

## Longevity notes

Grip strength is one of the most-studied correlates of healthy aging, and the dead hang builds it alongside shoulder health and spinal decompression. It's also zero-impact and self-limiting — you simply let go when you're done. Accumulate 2-3 minutes of total hang time per week; it doubles as a daily shoulder-health habit.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** Add 5-10 seconds per set when you can finish all sets without grip failure.
- **Progress to:** Single-arm hang, 15 seconds per side
- **Regress to:** Feet-supported hang (toes on a box, partial bodyweight)
