# Push-Up

## Details

- **Category:** Horizontal Push
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** push
- **Equipment:** Bodyweight
- **Primary muscles:** Chest, Triceps, Front Delts
- **Secondary muscles:** Abs

## Instructions

1. Start in a high plank: hands under shoulders, body in a straight line from head to heels, glutes and core braced.
2. Lower your chest toward the floor over two to three seconds by bending your elbows, keeping them about 45 degrees from your torso. Inhale on the way down.
3. Descend until your chest nearly touches the floor, or as deep as you can while keeping your hips level and your body in one line.
4. Push the floor away to return to the start in about one second, exhaling as you rise, fully extending your arms without shrugging your shoulders.
5. Work in sets of 5 to 15 reps with two reps in reserve. When 3 sets of 15 stay clean and slow, make it harder rather than longer.

## Cues

- Body straight as a plank from head to heels
- Elbows at roughly 45 degrees from your torso
- Two to three seconds down, one second up
- Chest to the floor, or as close as your strength allows
- Exhale as you push, squeeze your glutes so the hips stay level

## Common mistakes

- Hips sagging below the body line
- Flaring the elbows out to 90 degrees
- Only lowering halfway and calling it a rep
- Racing the reps so the body bounces instead of pressing
- Craning the neck to look forward instead of keeping it in line

## Safety

If you have a shoulder impingement or recent rotator-cuff issue, keep reps pain-free and reduce range before adding volume. Stop if you feel pinching at the front of the shoulder. Wrist discomfort is common — use push-up handles or fists, or elevate your hands on a bench, which also makes the movement easier. As with any plank position, stop the set when your hips start to sag: a clean half set beats a sloppy full one.

## Longevity notes

### Why this matters for a long life

Push-up capacity is one of the simplest field tests of upper-body health — studies link higher push-up counts with lower cardiovascular event risk in men. It is also the exact movement of getting yourself up off the floor, which is one of the first things people lose and one of the most useful to keep.

### Rhythm and tempo

Lower for two to three seconds, press up in about one. Sets of 5 to 15 with two reps in reserve, two or three times a week, build and hold strength without beating up your shoulders. Full range beats half reps: elevate your hands on a bench or wall to keep every rep honest rather than cutting depth.

### Keeping it for life

It needs no equipment and no gym, so it is the easiest strength habit to keep when life gets busy — a set before your shower counts. Progress by making the position harder (feet elevated, a weight vest, or a slower descent) rather than chasing endless reps.

> The floor is the one gym you always have with you.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** When you can do 3 sets of 15 clean reps, progress to feet-elevated push-ups or add a weighted vest.
- **Progress to:** Feet-elevated push-up
- **Regress to:** Incline push-up with hands on a bench or wall
