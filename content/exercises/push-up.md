# Push-Up

## Details

- **Category:** Horizontal Push
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** push
- **Equipment:** Bodyweight
- **Primary muscles:** Chest, Triceps, Front Delts
- **Secondary muscles:** Abs

## Instructions

1. Start in a high plank: hands under shoulders, body in a straight line from head to heels, glutes and core braced.
2. Lower your chest toward the floor by bending your elbows, keeping them about 45 degrees from your torso.
3. Descend until your chest nearly touches the floor, or as deep as you can while keeping your hips level.
4. Push the floor away to return to the start, fully extending your arms without shrugging your shoulders.

## Cues

- Body straight as a plank from head to heels
- Elbows at roughly 45 degrees from your torso
- Chest to the floor, or as close as your strength allows

## Common mistakes

- Hips sagging below the body line
- Flaring the elbows out to 90 degrees
- Only lowering halfway and calling it a rep

## Safety

If you have a shoulder impingement or recent rotator-cuff issue, keep reps pain-free and reduce range before adding volume. Stop if you feel pinching at the front of the shoulder.

## Longevity notes

Push-up capacity is one of the simplest field tests of upper-body health — studies link higher push-up counts with lower cardiovascular event risk in men. Beyond the numbers, it's the movement of getting yourself off the floor, and it trains the chest, shoulders, and triceps with zero equipment for life. Full range beats half reps; elevate your hands to keep every rep honest.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** When you can do 3 sets of 15 clean reps, progress to feet-elevated push-ups or add a weighted vest.
- **Progress to:** Feet-elevated push-up
- **Regress to:** Incline push-up with hands on a bench or wall
