# Inverted Row

## Details

- **Category:** Horizontal Pull
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** pull
- **Equipment:** Bodyweight
- **Primary muscles:** Lats, Upper Back, Rear Delts
- **Secondary muscles:** Biceps, Abs, Forearms
- **Aliases:** bodyweight row, supine row

## Instructions

1. Set a bar (or suspension-trainer handles) at about waist height. Lie underneath it and grip the bar with hands slightly wider than shoulder-width.
2. Walk your feet forward until your body hangs in a straight line, heels on the floor. The steeper your body angle, the harder the rep.
3. Pull your chest to the bar, driving your elbows back and squeezing your shoulder blades together.
4. Lower under control to straight arms. Keep your glutes braced so your body stays a rigid plank throughout.

## Cues

- Pull your chest to the bar, elbows driving back
- Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top
- Keep your body rigid — no sagging, no piking

## Common mistakes

- Hips sagging so the body bends at the waist
- Shrugging the shoulders up toward the ears
- Using momentum instead of a controlled pull

## Safety

Make sure the bar or anchor is secure before loading your full body weight. If you have an acute shoulder injury, keep the range pain-free and avoid the bottom stretch position.

## Longevity notes

Pulling strength balances all the pushing and sitting of modern life, and it's the foundation of a healthy upper back and posture. The inverted row is the most joint-friendly way to build it — your feet stay on the ground, so you can scale the difficulty precisely and train the pulling pattern for decades without beating up your shoulders. Strong lats and rhomboids are what keep you upright at 80.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** Lower the bar (steeper body angle) when you can do 3 sets of 12 with a 1-second pause at the top.
- **Progress to:** Pull-up or chin-up
- **Regress to:** Standing band row at chest height
