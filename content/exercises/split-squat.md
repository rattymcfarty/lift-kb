# Split Squat

## Details

- **Category:** Lunge
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** lunge
- **Equipment:** Bodyweight, Dumbbell
- **Primary muscles:** Quadriceps, Glutes
- **Secondary muscles:** Hamstrings, Calves, Abs
- **Aliases:** bulgarian split squat (rear-foot elevated variant)

## Instructions

1. Take a long staggered stance, one foot forward and one back, torso tall and core braced. Hold onto a wall or rack for balance if needed.
2. Lower straight down until your front thigh is roughly parallel to the floor and your back knee hovers just above the ground.
3. Keep most of your weight on the front leg; the back leg is a kickstand, not a driver.
4. Drive through your front foot to stand. Complete all reps on one side before switching.

## Cues

- Tall torso, ribs stacked over hips
- Front knee tracks over the middle of your foot
- Drive through the whole front foot to stand

## Common mistakes

- Front knee collapsing inward
- Torso leaning far forward over the front thigh
- Pushing off the back foot instead of driving through the front leg

## Safety

If you have acute knee pain, shorten the range to your pain-free zone and keep the front knee behind the toes initially. Use balance support freely — a fall during the exercise defeats its purpose.

## Longevity notes

Single-leg strength is balance, and balance is independence: falls are the leading cause of injury in older adults, and the split squat trains the exact pattern — one leg loaded, body stable — that keeps you on your feet. It also evens out left-right strength differences that bilateral squats hide. Start bodyweight, hold a wall, and earn your depth; the goal is a controlled single-leg squat you can still do at 75, not a wobbly weighted one today.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** Add dumbbells when you can do 3 sets of 10 per leg with a 2-second pause at the bottom, no balance assist.
- **Progress to:** Rear-foot-elevated (Bulgarian) split squat
- **Regress to:** Assisted split squat holding a doorframe or TRX
