# Dead Bug

## Details

- **Category:** Core
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** core
- **Equipment:** Bodyweight
- **Primary muscles:** Abs

## Instructions

1. Lie on your back with arms extended toward the ceiling and hips and knees bent to 90 degrees. Press your lower back flat into the floor.
2. Brace your core and slowly extend your right arm overhead while straightening your left leg, hovering both just above the floor.
3. Pause, then return to the start position with control. Your lower back must not arch at any point.
4. Repeat on the opposite side. That is one rep per side; alternate for the full set.

## Cues

- Press your lower back into the floor like you're squashing a grape
- Exhale as the opposite arm and leg extend
- Slow is the point — if it's easy, you're going too fast

## Common mistakes

- Letting the lower back arch off the floor
- Holding your breath instead of breathing behind the brace
- Moving the arms and legs too fast to feel the core working

## Safety

If you feel your lower back lifting off the floor, shorten the range — only extend as far as you can keep the back pinned down. Skip if you have an acute abdominal strain.

## Longevity notes

The dead bug trains the deep core the way your spine actually needs it — resisting extension under load, which is what protects your back when you lift, carry, and twist in real life. Unlike sit-ups, it builds stability without grinding the lumbar spine through repeated flexion. A quiet, braced trunk is the foundation every other lift in this library stands on.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** When you can do 3 sets of 10 per side with zero back arch, slow the tempo to 4 seconds per rep or add a light band.
- **Progress to:** Hollow-body hold
- **Regress to:** Supine 90-90 breathing with a light brace (no limb movement)
