# Ab Wheel

A rolling anti-extension core exercise that trains your abs and trunk to resist your lower back sagging as your arms reach overhead.

## Details

- **Category:** Core
- **Difficulty:** intermediate
- **Movement pattern:** core
- **Equipment:** Other
- **Primary muscles:** Abs

## Instructions

1. Kneel on a mat holding the wheel handles under your shoulders, hips slightly tucked and ribs pulled down.
2. Brace your abs and roll the wheel forward slowly, reaching only as far as you can keep your lower back flat.
3. Pause briefly at your furthest controlled point, keeping hips and shoulders moving together.
4. Pull the wheel back by drawing your ribs toward your hips, not by hiking your hips up.
5. Stop the set when your lower back starts to arch.

## Cues

- Ribs down, tailbone tucked
- Roll only as far as you can control
- Pull back with your abs

## Common mistakes

- Letting the lower back sag and arch
- Pushing the hips back instead of pulling with the abs
- Rolling out too far too soon

## Safety

Start with a short range or a wall-stop version. Skip it with a recent back or shoulder injury; stop if you feel pinching in the low back.

## Longevity notes

A strong, stable core protects the spine in every other movement and makes daily tasks — bending, twisting, reaching — safer. Core endurance matters more than core power.
