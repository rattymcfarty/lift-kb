# Goblet Squat

## Details

- **Category:** Squat
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** squat
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell, Kettlebell
- **Primary muscles:** Quadriceps, Glutes
- **Secondary muscles:** Abs, Upper Back

## Instructions

1. Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell vertically at chest height, elbows tucked under the weight.
2. Stand with feet slightly wider than shoulder-width, toes turned out 15-30 degrees.
3. Push your hips back and bend your knees, keeping your chest tall and the weight close to your body.
4. Descend until your elbows touch the insides of your knees, or as deep as you can without your lower back rounding.
5. Drive through your whole foot to stand, squeezing your glutes at the top.

## Cues

- Chest tall, weight close
- Knees track over toes
- Whole foot pressed into the floor

## Common mistakes

- Heels lifting off the floor
- Lower back rounding at the bottom of the rep
- Knees caving inward on the way up

## Safety

If you have a knee replacement or acute knee pain, limit depth to your pain-free range and check with your clinician first. Never bounce out of the bottom position.

## Longevity notes

The squat pattern is how you sit, stand, and get off the floor for the rest of your life — train it for depth and control, not maximal load. A deep, easy goblet squat you can still do at 80 beats a heavy barbell you abandon at 50. Keep 2-3 reps in reserve; joint-friendly volume beats grinding singles for healthspan.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** Add 2-4 kg when you can complete 3 sets of 12 with clean form at RPE 7 or less.
- **Progress to:** Double-kettlebell front squat
- **Regress to:** Bodyweight box squat to a chair or bench
