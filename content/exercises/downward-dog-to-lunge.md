# Downward Dog to Lunge

## Details

- **Category:** Mobility
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** mobility
- **Equipment:** Bodyweight
- **Primary muscles:** Hamstrings, Quadriceps
- **Secondary muscles:** Glutes

## Overview

Downward Dog to Lunge is a beginner mobility exercise for the hamstrings and quadriceps, performed with just your bodyweight. Regular mobility work keeps joints moving freely and makes strength training safer.

## Instructions

1. Start in downward dog.
2. Lift one leg, then step the foot through to the outside of the same hand into a deep lunge.
3. Sink the hips, keeping the back knee off or on the floor, feeling the hip-flexor stretch.
4. Step back to downward dog and repeat on the other side.

## Cues

- Move slowly, no forcing
- Breathe into the stretch
- Stay relaxed outside the target area

## Longevity notes

Mobility is use-it-or-lose-it. Ten minutes of daily joint work preserves the ranges that keep you independent — reaching, bending, and getting up from the floor.
