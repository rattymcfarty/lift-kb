# Farmer Carry

## Details

- **Category:** Carry
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** carry
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell, Kettlebell
- **Primary muscles:** Forearms, Upper Back
- **Secondary muscles:** Abs, Glutes, Quadriceps
- **Aliases:** farmers walk

## Instructions

1. Pick up a heavy dumbbell or kettlebell in each hand and stand tall.
2. Pull your shoulders slightly back and down, brace your core as if for a light punch.
3. Walk with short, controlled steps, keeping your torso upright — no leaning or swaying.
4. Set the weights down with control by hinging at the hips. Rest, then repeat.

## Cues

- Tall chest, ribs down
- Crush the handles
- Walk like you're balancing a book on your head

## Common mistakes

- Leaning away from the weights or shrugging excessively
- Shuffling with tiny rushed steps instead of controlled strides
- Holding your breath for the whole carry

## Safety

Clear your walking path first. If you have lower-back issues, start light and keep carries short; stop if you feel the need to lean sideways to compensate.

## Longevity notes

Carrying heavy things is a fundamental human movement — groceries, luggage, grandchildren. The farmer carry trains grip, posture, and core stability all at once, and it's self-regulating: your grip gives out long before anything dangerous happens. Walk 30-60 seconds per set, 2-3 times a week; your future self carrying groceries will thank you.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** Increase weight when you can carry for 60 seconds with perfect posture at RPE 7 or less.
- **Progress to:** Trap-bar carry or heavier dumbbells for distance
- **Regress to:** Single-arm suitcase carry with a light weight
