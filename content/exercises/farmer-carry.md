# Farmer Carry

## Details

- **Category:** Carry
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** carry
- **Equipment:** Dumbbell, Kettlebell
- **Primary muscles:** Forearms, Upper Back
- **Secondary muscles:** Abs, Glutes, Quadriceps
- **Aliases:** farmers walk

## Instructions

1. Pick up a heavy dumbbell or kettlebell in each hand and stand tall. Hinge at the hips to reach them, rather than bending your back over.
2. Pull your shoulders slightly back and down, brace your core as if for a light punch, and keep your ribs down over your hips.
3. Walk with short, controlled steps at a steady pace — roughly one step per second — keeping your torso upright and your shoulders level. No leaning, no swaying, no rushing.
4. Walk for 30 to 60 seconds, then set the weights down with control by hinging at the hips. Rest for 60 to 90 seconds and repeat for 3 to 5 carries.
5. Stop the carry when your grip or posture starts to fail — that is the natural end of the set, not a problem to fight through.

## Cues

- Tall chest, ribs down
- Crush the handles
- Walk like you're balancing a book on your head
- Short steps, steady rhythm — no rush
- Breathe: keep the brace, but don't hold your breath

## Common mistakes

- Leaning away from the weights or shrugging excessively
- Shuffling with tiny rushed steps instead of controlled strides
- Holding your breath for the whole carry
- Setting the weights down by rounding the back instead of hinging
- Going so heavy that posture collapses in the first ten metres

## Safety

Clear your walking path first, and choose a route where you can set the weights down safely if your grip goes. If you have lower-back issues, start light and keep carries short; stop if you feel the need to lean sideways to compensate. Grip fatigue is the built-in safety brake on this exercise — let it work, and do not use straps to push past a failing grip until your posture is rock solid.

## Longevity notes

### Why this matters for a long life

Carrying heavy things is a fundamental human movement — groceries, luggage, grandchildren, firewood. The farmer carry trains grip, posture and core stability all at once, and it is self-regulating: your grip gives out long before anything dangerous happens.

Grip strength itself is one of the more useful markers of healthy ageing, and carrying is the most direct way to train it without a gym.

### Rhythm and tempo

Steady and unhurried: about one step per second, 30 to 60 seconds per carry, 3 to 5 carries, two or three times a week. The tempo is the point — a fast, shuffling carry teaches your trunk nothing, while a slow, tall one keeps the whole chain working.

### Keeping it for life

It is one of the easiest movements to keep, because it looks exactly like the carrying you already do. Progress by adding time, then weight, then distance, and judge each carry by whether your posture survived it.

> Train the carry and the weekly shop stops being a chore you dread.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** Increase weight when you can carry for 60 seconds with perfect posture at RPE 7 or less.
- **Progress to:** Trap-bar carry or heavier dumbbells for distance
- **Regress to:** Single-arm suitcase carry with a light weight
