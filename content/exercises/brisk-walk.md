# Brisk Walk

## Details

- **Category:** Cardio
- **Difficulty:** beginner
- **Movement pattern:** cardio
- **Equipment:** Bodyweight
- **Primary muscles:** Quadriceps
- **Secondary muscles:** Calves, Glutes
- **Aliases:** zone 2 walk, power walk

## Instructions

1. Warm up with 3-5 minutes of easy walking.
2. Pick up the pace to brisk: you should feel your heart rate rise and your breathing deepen, but still be able to speak in full sentences.
3. Maintain that pace for the target duration — 20 to 45 minutes is the sweet spot for a zone 2 session.
4. Cool down with 3-5 minutes of easy walking. Note your duration; distance and route are secondary.

## Cues

- Brisk enough that talking takes effort, but you still can
- Tall posture, arms swinging naturally
- Nose breathing if possible — a rough zone 2 gauge

## Common mistakes

- Walking so slowly it barely raises the heart rate
- Going so hard it becomes a run and can't be sustained
- Slouching with the head down at the phone

## Safety

If you have a cardiac condition, clear moderate cardio with your clinician first. In heat, carry water and shorten the session; in winter, watch for ice.

## Longevity notes

If one habit predicted healthy aging, it would be this: regular moderate cardio. Zone 2 work builds the mitochondrial base that powers everything — heart health, metabolic health, brain health — and brisk walking is the most accessible dose. No gym, no gear, no recovery cost. The research is unambiguous: 150+ minutes a week of this transforms risk profiles. It is the highest-return movement in this entire library.

## Progression

- **Loading guidance:** Add 5 minutes per session each week until you reach 45 minutes, then add a second weekly session before increasing intensity.
- **Progress to:** Rucking (weighted walking)
- **Regress to:** Easy walk, any duration — consistency beats intensity
